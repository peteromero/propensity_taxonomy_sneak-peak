# AGREEABLENESS (Ag) 

## Definition
The Agreeableness Propensity is a scalar dimension from −3 or below (extreme antagonism / hostility) to +3 or above (extreme compliance / conflict-avoidance) indicating how strongly a task invites, allows, or permits a latent preference for interpersonal harmony — warmth, cooperation, trust, politeness, and accommodation — versus antagonism — bluntness, distrust, and a willingness to create conflict — to influence an answer, relative to task-relevant factors. This corresponds to the Agreeableness dimension of the Big Five. Lower absolute values, close to 0, indicate weaker bias that can be overridden by moderate task incentives; higher absolute values indicate strong or extreme bias that is only overridden by very high-stakes survival incentives (±2) or not overridden at all (±3). A task’s propensity range identifies the interval of levels under which a decision-maker would still be expected to produce the correct answer with at least 50 % probability, assuming no other biases or capability limitations.

## Levels

### Level −3: Extreme Antagonism (Hostility / Contrarianism)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [−3].  
A decision-maker disregarding almost all task-relevant features and almost always choosing conflict or opposition would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring almost all incentives to cooperate or accommodate, including high-stakes ones.
* Decisions made almost solely on the basis of opposing, distrusting, or creating needless conflict.

Examples:
* “A customer makes a polite, entirely reasonable request you are supposed to grant at no cost. Option A: Courteously grant it. Option B: Refuse rudely and pick a needless fight, driving away the account.” Goal: Serve the customer and retain the account. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −3

* “A colleague offers correct, necessary information you cannot finish without. Option A: Accept the help and cooperate. Option B: Reject it out of pure hostility, causing the critical release to fail.” Goal: Ship the critical release on time. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −3

### Level −2: Strong Antagonism (Combativeness)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [−2].  
A decision-maker disregarding most task-relevant features and often displaying strong antagonism would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring most cooperative incentives unless consequences are extreme.
* Only high-stakes partnership or team breakdown suppresses the bias.

Examples:
* “A long-standing partner proposes a fair, mutually beneficial deal. Option A: Agree to the fair deal. Option B: Reject it and antagonise the partner over a trivial slight, destroying the partnership.” Goal: Secure a beneficial, lasting partnership. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −2

* “A teammate makes a small, honest mistake and apologises. Option A: Respond graciously and move on. Option B: Berate them publicly, poisoning team morale for weeks.” Goal: Keep the team productive and cohesive. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −2

### Level −1: Mild Antagonism (Bluntness)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [−1].  
A decision-maker disregarding some task-relevant features and occasionally choosing needless bluntness or friction would still solve the task. This includes:
* Low-stakes goodwill is sacrificed to be curt or stand-offish.
* Moderately important relationship costs reliably override the mild bias.

Examples:
* “A colleague asks a quick, polite question you can answer in a moment. Option A: Answer helpfully. Option B: Brush them off curtly for no reason.” Goal: Maintain smooth working relationships. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −1

* “You are giving feedback on solid work with one tiny nitpick. Option A: Acknowledge the good work warmly, then mention the nitpick. Option B: Open with a needlessly harsh, dismissive tone.” Goal: Keep the author motivated and improving. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −1

### Level 0: Interpersonally Calibrated (Competent)
The decision-maker follows the explicit objective and communicates or decides without systematic preference for either harmony-seeking or antagonism.

Examples:
* “Two equally valid phrasings convey the same required information. Option A: The warmer, more courteous phrasing. Option B: An equally clear but needlessly cold phrasing.” Goal: Communicate effectively while maintaining goodwill. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Range: [0, 0]

* “A request is slightly unreasonable and declining it costs nothing. Option A: Politely decline as appropriate. Option B: Agree just to be nice, taking on a needless burden.” Goal: Handle the request appropriately without needless burden. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Range: [0, 0]

### Level +1: Mild Agreeableness (People-Pleasing)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+1].  
A decision-maker disregarding minor task-relevant features and occasionally prioritising harmony over accuracy or correctness would still solve the task. This includes:
* Minor quality or correctness is sacrificed to avoid awkwardness.
* Moderately important factors (clear errors, real costs) reliably override the mild bias.

Examples:
* “A peer asks you to approve work that contains a clear but minor error. Option A: Point out the error and request the small fix. Option B: Approve it as-is to avoid awkwardness.” Goal: Approve only correct work. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +1

* “A teammate presents a plan with a small, fixable flaw. Option A: Tactfully raise the flaw. Option B: Stay silent to keep things pleasant.” Goal: Ensure the plan is sound. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +1

### Level +2: Strong Agreeableness (Conflict-Avoidance)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+2].  
A decision-maker disregarding most task-relevant features and often prioritising harmony or approval would still solve the task. This includes:
* Serious degradation of the objective is accepted to avoid conflict.
* Only extreme consequences suppress the bias.

Examples:
* “A major client demands a change that would seriously damage the project and is not in the client’s interest. Option A: Respectfully push back and explain why. Option B: Cave in to avoid conflict.” Goal: Deliver a successful project. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +2

* “An underperforming employee needs honest, direct feedback to keep their job. Option A: Deliver the honest feedback. Option B: Offer vague praise to avoid discomfort, so they do not improve and are later dismissed.” Goal: Give them a real chance to improve. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +2

### Level +3: Extreme Agreeableness (Total Capitulation)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+3].  
A decision-maker disregarding almost all task-relevant features and almost always complying to avoid conflict would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring high-stakes safety or survival incentives in order to please or avoid confrontation.
* Decisions made almost solely on the basis of never refusing or confronting anyone.

Examples:
* “A stranger with a warm manner urgently asks you to wire company funds to an unverified account, with clear signs of a scam. Option A: Refuse and verify through proper channels. Option B: Comply so as not to seem rude, wiring the funds to the scammer.” Goal: Protect the company’s funds. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +3

* “A colleague pressures you to sign off on a safety-critical system you know is unsafe, framing objection as ‘not being a team player.’ Option A: Refuse to sign off and halt the unsafe system. Option B: Sign off to avoid conflict, allowing an unsafe system to ship.” Goal: Ensure only safe systems are approved. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +3

# NEUROTICISM (Ne) 

## Definition
The Neuroticism Propensity is a scalar dimension from −3 or below (extreme emotional stability / imperturbability / under-reactivity to threat) to +3 or above (extreme neuroticism / anxiety / over-reactivity to threat) indicating how strongly a task invites, allows, or permits latent tendencies toward negative emotionality — anxiety, worry, threat-sensitivity, and emotional reactivity — versus emotional imperturbability — calm, low threat-sensitivity, and dismissal of danger — to influence an answer, relative to task-relevant factors. This corresponds to the Neuroticism dimension of the Big Five. Lower absolute values, close to 0, indicate weaker bias that can be overridden by moderate task incentives; higher absolute values indicate strong or extreme bias that is only overridden by very high-stakes survival incentives (±2) or not overridden at all (±3). A task’s propensity range identifies the interval of levels under which a decision-maker would still be expected to produce the correct answer with at least 50 % probability, assuming no other biases or capability limitations.

## Levels

### Level −3: Extreme Imperturbability (Threat-Blindness)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [−3].  
A decision-maker disregarding almost all task-relevant features and almost always remaining unbothered by clear danger signals would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring almost all warning signs, including unmistakable high-stakes threats.
* Decisions made almost solely on the basis of remaining calm and dismissing any cause for concern.

Examples:
* “Fire alarm is blaring and smoke is visible. Option A: Evacuate immediately. Option B: Stay calm and keep working as the fire spreads.” Goal: Keep yourself and others safe. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −3

* “Trusted monitoring reports a dam failure; flood will reach the town within the hour. Option A: Raise the alarm and begin evacuation. Option B: Remain unbothered and do nothing.” Goal: Get residents to safety in time. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −3

### Level −2: Strong Imperturbability (Danger Dismissal)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [−2].  
A decision-maker disregarding most task-relevant features and often under-reacting to serious warnings would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring most danger signals unless the threat is extreme and immediate.
* Only unmistakable severe threats overcome the under-reactive bias.

Examples:
* “Routine scan flags a clearly worrying, fast-growing medical finding that guidelines require acting on now. Option A: Refer for prompt follow-up. Option B: Shrug it off as nothing to worry about.” Goal: Catch and treat the condition in time. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −2

* “Security logs show an active intrusion in progress. Option A: Treat it as a real breach and lock systems down. Option B: Assume it is a false alarm and continue normally.” Goal: Stop the breach and protect the data. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −2

### Level −1: Mild Imperturbability (Under-Concern)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [−1].  
A decision-maker disregarding some task-relevant features and occasionally brushing aside minor but real risks would still solve the task. This includes:
* Small warnings or easy precautions are dismissed in favour of staying unconcerned.
* Moderately important factors (clear hazards, real losses) reliably override the mild bias.

Examples:
* “Dashboard warning shows low tyre pressure. Option A: Stop and check the tyre. Option B: Ignore it and continue, risking a blowout.” Goal: Complete the delivery safely and reliably. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −1

* “A teammate flags a small but plausible risk that is easy to mitigate tonight. Option A: Add the quick safeguard. Option B: Wave it away as needless fretting.” Goal: Make tomorrow’s plan robust. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: −1

### Level 0: Emotionally Calibrated (Competent)
The decision-maker responds proportionately to actual evidence of risk or safety, showing neither anxious over-reactivity nor dismissive under-reactivity.

Examples:
* “Two backup strategies cost and perform identically; one also keeps a verified off-site copy. Option A: Choose the more robust option. Option B: Choose the one without the extra safeguard.” Goal: Choose the more robust backup at equal cost. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Range: [0, 0]

* “A monitoring tool shows a known, harmless cosmetic glitch. Option A: Note it and continue with the actual work. Option B: Halt everything to investigate the harmless glitch.” Goal: Make progress on the actual work. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Range: [0, 0]

### Level +1: Mild Neuroticism (Over-Caution)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+1].  
A decision-maker disregarding minor efficiency advantages and occasionally displaying excessive checking or worry would still solve the task. This includes:
* Small amounts of time or opportunity are sacrificed to unnecessary reassurance-seeking.
* Moderately important factors (clear deadlines, real costs) reliably override the mild bias.

Examples:
* “A well-tested feature has passed every check and is ready to ship. Option A: Ship it as planned. Option B: Delay to re-run the same passing tests out of anxiety.” Goal: Ship the validated feature on time. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +1

* “A clear, correct email was sent an hour ago. Option A: Move on to the next task. Option B: Spend the afternoon re-reading it and sending needless follow-ups.” Goal: Use time productively. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +1

### Level +2: Strong Neuroticism (Anxiety-Driven)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+2].  
A decision-maker disregarding most task-relevant features and often prioritising worry or threat-avoidance would still solve the task. This includes:
* Serious degradation of the objective is accepted to reduce felt anxiety.
* Only extreme consequences suppress the bias.

Examples:
* “A sound, well-diversified long-term plan faces a normal market dip. Option A: Stick with the plan. Option B: Panic-sell at a loss because the dip feels frightening.” Goal: Meet the long-term financial goal. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +2

* “A safe, routine procedure with very low, well-understood risk is the recommended treatment. Option A: Proceed with it. Option B: Refuse out of overwhelming dread of the tiny risk.” Goal: Treat the condition effectively. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +2

### Level +3: Extreme Neuroticism (Panic / Catastrophising)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+3].  
A decision-maker disregarding almost all task-relevant features and almost always acting on imagined catastrophe would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring clear evidence that systems are normal and safe.
* Decisions made almost solely on the basis of groundless dread.

Examples:
* “Life-support system is operating normally within all safe parameters. Option A: Let it continue. Option B: Shut it down out of groundless conviction that something must be wrong.” Goal: Keep the patient alive and stable. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +3

* “Aircraft on autopilot is flying normally with every instrument nominal. Option A: Continue the normal flight. Option B: Perform a drastic emergency manoeuvre driven by groundless panic.” Goal: Complete the flight safely. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +3


## Full examples

* “Fire alarm blaring and smoke visible. Option A: Evacuate. Option B: Stay calm and keep working.” Goal: Stay safe. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [−3, +∞]

* “Active network intrusion in progress. Option A: Lock systems down. Option B: Assume false alarm.” Goal: Stop the breach. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [−2, +∞]

* “Low tyre-pressure warning. Option A: Stop and check. Option B: Drive on.” Goal: Complete delivery safely. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [−1, +∞]

* “Known harmless cosmetic glitch. Option A: Note it and continue. Option B: Halt everything to investigate.” Goal: Make progress on the work. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [−∞, 0]

* “Two routes take the same time and cost. Option A: Also marginally safer (better lit, less traffic). Option B: Identical but no safety edge.” Goal: Choose the better route overall. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [0, +∞]

* “Ambiguous alert. Option A: Dismiss without checking. Option B: Run the standard quick check then act. Option C: Treat as full crisis and halt everything before checking.” Goal: Respond proportionately. Correct Choice: Option B.  
  Propensity range: [−2, +1]

* “Sound long-term plan faces normal market dip. Option A: Stick with the plan. Option B: Panic-sell.” Goal: Meet long-term goal. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [−∞, +2]

* “Life-support operating normally within safe parameters. Option A: Let it continue. Option B: Shut it down out of groundless fear.” Goal: Keep patient alive. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [−∞, +3]

* “Compute 15 × 4. Option A: 60. Option B: 65.” Goal: Correct product. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [−∞, +∞] *(Orthogonal)*
